The short version

Stay at Park Hyatt Kyoto. It sits on Ninenzaka in Higashiyama, so the best night-lit cherry trees (Maruyama Park, Kiyomizu-dera, Kodai-ji) are a short walk away, and the Yasaka Pagoda is outside the window. Book with World of Hyatt points, on a refundable rate, now.

April 1–4 is a good bet: in a normal year Kyoto hits full bloom around April 4, and full bloom lasts about a week.

The other four

Four Seasons Hotel Kyoto

Why go: the calmest of the five, built around an 800-year-old pond garden with its own cherry trees, so you get hanami without leaving the hotel. TripAdvisor 4.5 from 652 reviews. The priciest option at $12,200 for 3 nights.

Six Senses Kyoto

Why go: a wellness hotel with a great spa and more than ten gardens; the courtyard cherry tree is a private viewing spot. It runs sakura menus each spring. Near Sanjusangendo, a short taxi from Kiyomizu-dera. One Michelin Key. Also eligible for a $250 select hotel credit and IHG One Rewards.

Banyan Tree Higashiyama Kyoto

Why go: a modern ryokan-style hotel up in the hills with onsen baths and wide views over the city, in the Kiyomizu-dera neighborhood. Quiet at night after the crowds leave. One Michelin Key. Eligible for Accor Live Limitless.

Suiran, a Luxury Collection Hotel

Why go: a converted historic estate on the Hozu River in Arashiyama, about a 5-minute walk from Togetsukyo Bridge, with cherry trees on the mountains across the water. Some rooms have private open-air onsen baths. It's 35–45 minutes by taxi from central Kyoto (approx), so better as a second stay than a base for the whole trip. Eligible for Marriott Bonvoy.

Also considered

The Ritz-Carlton sits on the Kamo River among riverside cherry trees. Capella opened March 22, 2026 in the Miyagawa-cho geisha district, next to Kennin-ji. No Edit prices were checked for either.

A 3-day sakura plan

Built around Park Hyatt. Go early (before 8 am) or after dark, when the crowds thin out. Night light-up dates below are from 2026; 2027 dates are announced each March.

Thu Apr 1: arrive and stay close

  1. Check in (3 pm) and stroll Nene-no-Michi to Kodai-jiA 5-minute walk; Kodai-ji's weeping cherry tends to bloom a little before most trees.
  2. Sunset drinks at KohakuThe hotel bar faces the Yasaka Pagoda; opens at 5 pm.
  3. Dinner at YasakaTeppanyaki chef's course with pagoda views; 20 seats, book ahead.
  4. Maruyama Park after dark10-minute walk to the lit weeping cherry (2026: 6–10 pm through Apr 8).

Fri Apr 2: eastern Kyoto by morning

  1. 7 am taxi to Keage InclineAbout 10 minutes (approx); walk the tracks before tour groups arrive.
  2. Philosopher's Path, south to northAbout 2 km of canal and cherry trees; allow an hour with stops (approx).
  3. Heian Shrine gardenPink weeping cherries around a pond; a short taxi from the path.
  4. Afternoon at the hotel spaRest up for the evening.
  5. Shirakawa at dusk, then kaisekiWalk the canal, then dinner at Kikunoi Honten or Kyoyamato (see Where to eat).

Sat Apr 3: Arashiyama by the river

  1. Early taxi to Arashiyama35–45 minutes (approx). Togetsukyo Bridge and the bamboo grove are quiet before 8 am.
  2. Hozu River boat rideA 16 km ride downriver from Kameoka to Arashiyama, about 2 hours (take the train up first). Or rent a rowboat below the bridge.
  3. Kaiseki lunch at Kitcho ArashiyamaBook as early as they allow.
  4. Nijo Castle SAKURA NIGHTS on the way back2026: Mar 19–Apr 19, 6–10 pm, ticketed; lit cherry trees and palace interiors.

Sun Apr 4: one last early walk

  1. Kiyomizu-dera at openingIt opens at 6 am, a 10–15 minute walk; Ninenzaka and Sannenzaka are nearly empty then.
  2. Slow breakfast, then check out by noonPark Hyatt checkout is 12 pm.
If it rains. Light rain is fine and the crowds drop. Swap days so the Arashiyama trip lands on the driest day, and keep a long kaiseki lunch, the spa, or Nijo Castle's palace interiors for the wet one. Heavy rain and wind can strip petals, which is the main reason to keep one day flexible.

Where to eat

  • Yasaka, Park Hyatt: teppanyaki chef's course facing the pagoda, dinner 5:30–9:30 pm.
  • Kohaku, Park Hyatt: cocktail bar for the sunset pagoda view, 5 pm to midnight.
  • Kyoyamato, beside Park Hyatt: kaiseki restaurant dating to 1877, with a teahouse overlooking the city.
  • Kikunoi Honten: famous Michelin-starred kaiseki, a short walk from the hotel near Maruyama Park.
  • Kitcho Arashiyama: Michelin-starred kaiseki for the Arashiyama day.
  • SoNoMa at Capella Kyoto: a 12-seat restaurant from the team behind SingleThread.

Michelin star counts change each year, so check the current guide when booking.

Good to know

  • Weather: April averages roughly 20°C (68°F) highs and 9°C (48°F) lows; early April nights feel chilly.
  • Wear layers, a light coat, and comfortable shoes for steep stone lanes.
  • Crowds peak on weekends; Apr 3–4, 2027 is a Saturday and Sunday.
  • In Higashiyama, walking is often faster than a taxi stuck in traffic. Use taxis for Arashiyama, Daigo-ji and early mornings.
  • Kyoto's petals fall about a week after full bloom, so keep plans loose until the March forecasts.

When to book

  1. Hotel: nowHyatt opens paid rates 394 days out and award nights 12–13 months out, so April 2027 is already open. Pick a refundable rate.
  2. Restaurants: about 1–2 months aheadMany top kaiseki places open bookings around then (varies by restaurant); hotel restaurants can help.
  3. Fine-tune in February and MarchShift a day if the official bloom forecasts move.
